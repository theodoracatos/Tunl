Same Cave, a Different Mix: Sector Motifs and a Second Soundtrack

I love this game, and I have flown it more than anyone. That is exactly the problem: it had started to feel the same every time. Version 19.0 adds variety from sector 4 on, without a single new hazard and without making the start any harder.

Devlog · 9 October 2026 · all posts

Before changing anything I wrote down why it felt samey, straight from the code. Every run opens with the same 28 seconds, because the first sectors are the lesson. After that, every sector was the same recipe: a little of everything, a little denser each time. The music was one 27-second loop that played two and a half times in every good run. And the daily cave already had four hidden day types that nudged the densities, but nobody could tell them apart.

A motif per sector

From sector 4 (score 252) every sector now draws a motif: a different mix of the hazards that already exist. A crystal forest is dense with crystals and pairs, with fewer mines and no cannons. A minefield is the opposite. A gold vein thins the hazards and pours out coins. A boulder field brings more rock islands, a cannon alley more cannons, a rockfall more falling crystals, and a dark stretch turns the lights off except around your ship. About half of the sectors stay mixed, the way they were, so that a motif has something to stand out against.

The sector sign names it, for example SECTOR 6 · MINEFIELD. The title screen names the day type, and that day type makes its own motif come up twice as often.

Still one cave for everyone. The motif of a sector is computed from the date and the sector number, nothing else. It draws no random numbers and depends on no screen size, so every player on Earth flies the same sequence, and the cave test still builds an identical cave on six device sizes (how the daily cave works).

Different, not harder

Two rules came first. Nothing below score 233 may get harder, because that is where almost every real run ends, and a test now checks that every number below sector 4 is unchanged. And a motif changes the mix, not the pressure. That second rule needed measuring. The same simulated pilot I use for screen fairness flew 40 days of caves, 8 runs per skill tier, once with motifs and once without, on the same days and seeds.

The first version was too kind. Each motif made its own hazard denser and the others much thinner, and in sum that made strong runs about 10% longer. It also raised the coin supply by 11%, through a coupling I had forgotten: fewer crystals let more coin candidates through, which would have made the star cheaper. So a motif is now mostly the normal mix with one hazard turned up, and each motif corrects its own coins.

A second soundtrack, on the beat

From sector 4 the music changes to a second track by the same composer. The two tracks have different tempos, 140 and about 87 beats per minute, so they can never play at the same time without sounding like a mistake. My first hand-over overlapped them for about a second, and it did not survive the first listen. Now the main track waits for the start of its next two-bar phrase, sinks into a closing filter over its last bar and stops exactly on the line. The new track starts on its first beat, muffled, and opens up over half a bar, like coming up out of the dark. When a run ends in the deep, the music collapses and the second track plays its real ending, starting on its last hits.

And the first thing you see

The launch screen of the apps was black. It now glows in night violet, the colour of the app icon, fading to black at the edges so it runs into the dark title without a flash.

If you usually stop around sector 3, nothing has changed for you. If you get further, the cave is no longer the same cave twice.
