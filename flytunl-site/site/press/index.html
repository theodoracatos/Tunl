Boilerplate

Short (one line)

TUNL is a hold-to-thrust cave flyer for iOS, Android and the web where every player on Earth flies the exact same procedurally-generated cave each day.

Long (two paragraphs)

TUNL is a one-tap arcade flyer: hold to thrust up, release to fall, thread a narrowing cave while dodging stalactites, mines, and wall-mounted cannons. Coins widen the tunnel and unlock power-ups - a shield, a magnet, bullet time - so surviving deeper is a skill loop, not just a longer wait.

The catch is that the cave itself is seeded from the calendar date, so every player, on every platform, flies the identical corridor on a given day - the same near-miss, the same lucky coin run, the same wall that ends it. A daily leaderboard, a beatable ghost of the day's best run, and a one-tap share card turn that shared cave into something worth comparing notes on, not just a personal best to chase alone.

Plain text, no formatting - copy either block directly.

Feature list

  • A daily cave shared by every player. The level is calculated from the UTC date, so a score means the same thing on every phone, and the global leaderboard compares like with like.
  • One control. Hold to thrust, release to fall. Holding sets acceleration, not position, so the ship carries momentum.
  • Eight coins that show what they do. Gold widens the corridor, an hourglass slows time, a shield blocks a hit, a crosshair loads ammo, a magnet pulls coins, a bomb clears danger, and two look-alike traps, a poison flask and a drain whirlpool, take from you.
  • A run built as a plan. Seven-second sectors each introduce one hazard: stalactites, mines, boulders, chicanes, cannons, falling spikes.
  • A warp portal that makes you untouchable for a rush of speed.
  • Eight ships with trade-offs, mastery per ship, and six cosmetic paint finishes.
  • Ghost of your best run flying beside you, a debriefing with your world rank, and a share card that draws the tunnel you actually flew.
  • Daily missions, achievements and streaks on Game Center and Play Games.
  • Every run opens over a city at dusk and dives into the tunnel, and the ship is drawn in a three-quarter view with swing wings.

Story angles

Beyond a review, these are the parts of TUNL that make good stories. Each has a devlog post with the details and the numbers.

  • A level that is a function, not a file. How one date becomes the same cave for every player, and the two bugs that silently broke that promise. One Cave, Every Player
  • Balancing with simulated players. A difficulty plan tested by thousands of bot runs at four skill levels, and what that showed. Seven-Second Sectors
  • Fairness across hardware. Physics that is identical from 12 to 144 Hz and a shared leaderboard that survives very different screens. The Same Ship on Every Screen
  • One player comment, one constant. How the feel of the ship was tuned after real feedback. Too Fast, Then Too Floaty
  • 3D without a 3D engine. A Three-Quarter View on a Flat Canvas
